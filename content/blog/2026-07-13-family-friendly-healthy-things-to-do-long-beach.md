Long Beach is an unusually good city for raising active kids. Between a calm, lifeguarded bay, a five-mile beachfront, a working nature center, and a historic ranch with real barnyard animals, most of the best family outings here are outdoors, free or cheap, and naturally built around movement — walking, swimming, biking, climbing — rather than a screen. If you're trying to build healthy habits as a family, you don't need a program. You need a rotation. Here's ours.

## Naples & Belmont Shore: The Calm-Water Anchors

Start with **Mother's Beach**, officially Marine Park, tucked on the northeastern side of **Naples Island** at 5839 Appian Way. Because it sits inside protected Alamitos Bay below Marine Stadium, the water stays calm and shallow year-round — no open-ocean waves, no surprise currents — which is exactly why it's the default first-beach for Long Beach families with toddlers. A full playground sits right at the north end of the sand, close enough that parents can watch a kid go from splashing to climbing to swinging without losing sight of them. Add grass lawns for a picnic, a concession stand, restrooms, and paid lot parking (street parking nearby fills fast on weekends), and you have a complete, low-effort beach morning.

A short drive into **East Long Beach** gets you to **Colorado Lagoon** (5119 E Colorado St), the other calm-water option. The lagoon itself is shallow and wave-free, ideal for younger kids who aren't ready for the bay or ocean, and the surrounding park underwent a full renovation with an inclusive playground split into zones for ages 2–5 and 5–12 — slides, swings, climbing walls, and a role-play "general store" structure, all on accessible pathways. It's a quieter, less crowded alternative to Mother's Beach on a busy weekend.

## Downtown & Shoreline Village: The Half-Day Anchors

**Aquarium of the Pacific** (100 Aquarium Way, Downtown) is Long Beach's biggest family draw for a reason — 11,000-plus animals across more than 50 exhibits, hands-on touch pools in Shark Lagoon, and a Lorikeet Forest where kids feed the birds. It's open daily, 9 a.m. to 6 p.m., with free admission for children age two and under and discounted tickets for ages 3–11; booking online in advance is consistently cheaper than paying at the gate. Pair the visit with a walk: the Aquarium sits directly on the **Shoreline Pedestrian Bikepath**, a flat, car-free path that runs from Shoreline Village around Rainbow Lagoon.

That's also where **Wheel Fun Rentals** (429 Shoreline Village Dr) comes in. Beyond standard bikes, they rent four-wheeled surreys that seat up to six and kid trailers with a secured harness and UV cover — genuinely the easiest way to get younger kids moving on wheels without anyone falling off a bike. A self-guided ride from Shoreline Village toward Naples and Seal Beach turns errand-free family time into a few miles of exercise nobody complains about.

## Bixby Hill: A Free Walk Through Living History

For something slower, **Rancho Los Alamitos** (6400 E Bixby Hill Rd) is a free, walkable 19th-century ranch with gardens and a working barnyard — horses, sheep, goats, chickens, ducks, and rabbits. It's open for drop-in tours Wednesday through Sunday, 1–5 p.m. (last tour around 4 p.m.), admission and parking are free, and from April through August the ranch runs monthly kids' arts-and-crafts programming alongside the animal visits. It's the rare Long Beach outing that's entirely free and still fills two hours.

## Building It Into a Family Yoga Habit

If your family already likes movement together, Long Beach has room to bring yoga into the mix without turning it clinical. The city's flagship free session, **Yoga on the Bluff**, meets daily at 11 a.m. at the Bixby Park Annex above Alamitos Beach — open to all levels, donation-based, and a genuinely good first yoga class for a curious kid alongside a parent. Studios like **YogaSix** in the Marina Center also run broader family and beginner class schedules if you want a more structured weekly rhythm, taught by yoga teachers in a typical group-class format.

Worth knowing the vocabulary distinction, because the two services aren't interchangeable. A Saturday-morning family session at a local studio is a yoga class taught by a yoga teacher, built for general movement and fun. Yoga therapy is different: it's the clinical work our yoga therapists at Long Beach School of Yoga do one-on-one with adults managing a diagnosed health condition, under a treatment plan rather than a class schedule. Both have real value for a household — they just aren't the same service, and it helps to know which one you're booking.
