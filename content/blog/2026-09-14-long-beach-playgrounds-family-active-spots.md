Long Beach's first family-friendly guide covered the big anchors — the Aquarium, Mother's Beach, Colorado Lagoon, Rancho Los Alamitos. This one goes to the neighborhood level: the playgrounds locals actually drive to on a random Tuesday, plus a weekends-only mini theme park most visitors never hear about. All of it is free except the train rides, and all of it gets kids moving instead of scrolling.

## Why Playgrounds Belong on a Healthy Long Beach List

A playground is the simplest form of youth exercise infrastructure a city can build — climbing, swinging, and running games develop the same balance, grip strength, and cardiovascular base that adult fitness programs spend years rebuilding. Long Beach has leaned into this over the past several years, rebuilding a wave of playgrounds with themed play structures, shade cover, and ADA-accessible equipment so kids of different abilities can play side by side. None of what follows requires a membership, a reservation, or a fee (with one exception below).

## Admiral Kidd Park — Downtown's Nautical Playground

**Admiral Kidd Park** (2125 Santa Fe Ave, Downtown Long Beach) leans hard into its naval namesake — Rear Admiral Isaac C. Kidd, who served in World War II — with a play area built around a large rocking boat, a periscope, portholes, and a spinning ship's wheel. A double-seater swing and a zipline round out the equipment, all set on rubberized flooring under shade structures. The park has a specific focus on ADA accessibility, including features designed for kids who use wheelchairs or other mobility devices, which makes it one of the more genuinely inclusive play spaces in the city.

## Recreation Park's Dinosaur Playground

Long Beach's **Recreation Park** (4900 E 7th St, near the Traffic Circle) already shows up on this site's parks guide for its golf courses and pools, but the playground itself deserves its own mention. It reopened after a significant renovation with a full dinosaur theme — a "Dino Excavating Zone" and a T-Rex climbing structure — built for kids ages 5 to 12. The rebuild also increased ADA accessibility, including American Sign Language elements to support visually impaired visitors. Spinning swings and merry-go-rounds round out the equipment, and the surrounding lawn and trees make it an easy spot for a family picnic while the kids climb.

## Cherry/Junipero Beach — A Playground With Sand Attached

Tucked underneath the Long Beach Museum of Art and Bluff Park in **Alamitos Beach**, the **Cherry/Junipero Beach** playground is one of the newer additions to the city's play infrastructure. Two tall rope-web climbing structures anchor the space, along with tube slides and swings set on rubberized mats — and if the kids want more, an entire beach starts one step off the mat. The playground connects directly to the Saltwater Deck eatery, and sits across from public basketball and pickleball courts, with the Shoreline Pedestrian Bikepath running right past it. It's an easy stop to combine with a Bluff Park walk from the This Week section of our main directory guide.

## Lincoln Park — Downtown's Activity Hub

**Lincoln Park** (101 Pacific Ave, Downtown Long Beach) reopened as a genuine multi-activity hub: the playground sits in the middle of a skate park, a dog park, a turf sports field, a disc golf course, and a basketball court, all on rubberized matting with climbing equipment, bumpy slides, swings, and seesaws. There's also adult fitness equipment on-site — a small but real nod to multi-generational activity in one park. The park borders the Billie Jean King Main Library at the corner of Ocean Boulevard and Pacific Avenue, so a library stop is a natural add-on for a rainy or overheated day.

## Whaley Park — Space for a Range of Ages

Near Cal State Long Beach, **Whaley Park** (5620 E Atherton St) offers one of the more generously sized play areas in the city, with brightly colored spinning swings, climbing structures, ladders, and slides spread across a grassy, tree-shaded lot with picnic tables. A separate gated play area for younger children sits adjacent, making it easier for parents managing kids at different ages and stages. The playground sits behind a community center, which buffers it from the somewhat busy street out front.

## El Dorado Frontier — A Weekend Mini Theme Park Inside a Regional Park

The one item on this list that isn't entirely free: **El Dorado Frontier** (7550 E Spring St, East Long Beach, inside El Dorado East Regional Park) is a small Gold Rush–themed amusement park open **Saturdays and Sundays only, 11 a.m.–5 p.m.** Attractions include the Ruvolo Valley Train Station (a roughly 15-minute train ride that loops the park and passes through a strobe-lit mine shaft), the Crystal Corral Carousel, a gold-panning activity, and a 110-foot slide. A ticket covers park entry plus one carousel ride and one train ride per guest. It's a low-key, walkable counterpoint to bigger regional theme parks, and it sits inside the same park complex as the El Dorado Nature Center trails covered in our hikes guide — easy to combine a nature walk with a train ride in the same visit. Call ahead for current pricing and any holiday-hours changes; the park also runs a seasonal pumpkin patch in the fall.

## Two Family Programs Worth Bookmarking (Not This Week)

Two Long Beach youth-fitness programs are worth knowing about even though neither is actively enrolling this month. The **Long Beach Fire Department's Junior Lifeguard Program** (Claremont Beach, 5347 E Ocean Blvd) runs each summer for ages 9–17, teaching rescue technique, first aid, ocean sports, and physical conditioning over a six-week course that ends with the Ironguard event — a four-mile run and two-mile swim. And **Supertri Long Beach**, a beginner-friendly sprint triathlon, runs each July with youth-accessible distances. Both are summer-only; if your family wants in, the move now is to bookmark them for next year's registration window rather than look for a fall session that doesn't exist.
