Our first pass at [Long Beach's yoga scene](/blog/2026-08-03-best-yoga-movement-studios-long-beach) covered the free bluff class, the donation-based studio, and the hot-yoga corridor on PCH. What it didn't get to is everything else locals mean when they say "movement studio" — heated national-chain yoga, reformer Pilates, and barre. Long Beach has all three, spread from the Traffic Circle to Belmont Shore to the CSULB corridor, and none of it made the first list. Here's Part 2.

A quick vocabulary note, same as last time: what's below is community fitness — group classes anyone can drop into — not clinical **yoga therapy**, the one-on-one practice for a diagnosed health condition that we cover on our [yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy) page. Different tools, same goal of a healthier Long Beach.

## Heated National Yoga: CorePower Yoga at the Traffic Circle

**CorePower Yoga — The Circle** (4640 Pacific Coast Hwy) sits in the Sprouts-anchored Circle Marina Center right off the **Long Beach Traffic Circle**, serving the **Los Altos** and Circle neighborhoods a few miles inland from the beach studios. It's the heated-room, national-chain counterpart to West Coast Sweat's independent hot-yoga model: showers, hair dryers, lockers, mat and towel rentals, and a full lineup of heated vinyasa and yoga sculpt classes. New students get a free week of unlimited classes with no credit card required, which makes it a low-risk way to compare a big-studio heated practice against the independent options in Belmont Shore before committing to a membership (single classes run $34 drop-in, or class packs starting at $159 for five). For anyone who lives inland and doesn't want to fight Traffic Circle congestion to get to PCH, it's the closest heated-yoga option to that corridor.

## Reformer Pilates: LiveMetta, Club Pilates Marina & Club Pilates East Long Beach

Pilates is the fastest-growing addition to Long Beach's movement scene, and three reformer studios now cover the city west to east.

**LiveMetta Pilates + Fitness** (6539 E Pacific Coast Hwy, **Belmont Shore**) has quietly served the neighborhood for more than 20 years and is the most complete single studio on this list — reformer classes across four levels (from a free beginner-friendly Reformer Orientation up through the advanced, invitation-gated Reformer Power), mat Pilates, and non-Pilates fitness classes including rebounding (trampoline cardio) and dumbbell-based Total Body Strength. It's a good fit for someone who wants one membership that covers both a Pilates practice and a straightforward strength or cardio day.

**Club Pilates Long Beach Marina** (6236 Pacific Coast Hwy) sits in the same Marina Pacifica cluster as Ra Yoga and West Coast Sweat from our first guide, so it's an easy add-on for anyone already doing PCH-corridor studio hopping — weekend hours start at 7 a.m., weekday mornings as early as 6 a.m. **Club Pilates East Long Beach** (5939 E Spring St, near **CSULB**) runs the same reformer-based curriculum — eight class types across four levels, from beginner Foundation through the advanced Mastery class — for the east-side campus corridor, open as early as 5:30 a.m. on weekdays. Both, like every Club Pilates location, offer a free 30-minute intro class with a certified instructor before you commit to a package.

## Barre: The Bar Method Near CSULB

**The Bar Method Long Beach** (3891 Lakewood Blvd, Suite 500, near **CSULB** and the Los Altos corridor) is the city's dedicated barre studio, marking its parent brand's 25th year in 2026. The signature format uses body weight, a ballet barre, and light props for small-range, high-rep isometric work aimed at long, lean muscle rather than bulk — with modifications built in for prenatal clients and injuries. The studio itself was renovated recently into an open-concept locker room and retail space with a hydration station, and it's open seven days a week with free parking, which is a real point of friction removed compared to paid-lot studios closer to the beach. Reservations are required and there's no walk-in policy, so first-timers should book online before showing up.

## How These Compare to Part 1

If Part 1 was about the beach-adjacent, heat-and-community end of Long Beach movement — free bluff yoga, donation-based classes, PCH hot yoga — Part 2 is the structured, equipment-based end: heated national-brand yoga at the Traffic Circle, reformer Pilates from Belmont Shore to CSULB, and barre near the university corridor. Between the two guides, a Long Beach resident can build a full weekly rotation without leaving a roughly five-mile stretch of the city. Most of the studios above offer some version of a free first class or intro week — the cheapest way to figure out which format actually sticks before paying for a membership.
