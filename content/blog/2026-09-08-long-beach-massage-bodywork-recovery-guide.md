Long Beach's recovery scene has mostly been a heat-and-cold story: infrared saunas, contrast-therapy suites, and cold plunges up and down Pacific Coast Highway. Our earlier guide to [cold plunge and sauna studios](/blog/2026-06-29-cold-plunge-sauna-recovery-long-beach) covers that side well. But recovery isn't only temperature — it's also hands-on, and Long Beach has a quieter bodywork scene built around chiropractic-adjacent massage and assisted stretch therapy that doesn't get the same attention. This guide fills in that half of the picture, with stops in Bixby Knolls, the Marina, and East Long Beach.

## Chiropractic-Rooted Bodywork in Bixby Knolls, Long Beach

**Bixby Knolls Wellness Center** (4301 Atlantic Ave, Ste 5) is the neighborhood's one-stop recovery address. Opened by Dr. Anna Pearl, D.C. in 2018, the practice pairs gentle chiropractic adjustments with therapeutic massage, cold laser therapy, and acupuncture, and also runs yoga, Pilates, and personal training out of the same space — useful if you'd rather consolidate a recovery routine under one roof than drive between five studios. All of the doctors are Webster-certified for prenatal care, which matters if you're pregnant and looking for bodywork that's actually trained for it rather than generically "pregnancy-friendly." Hours run Tuesday–Thursday 8 a.m.–7 p.m. and Friday–Saturday 8:30 a.m.–4 p.m., with a split Monday schedule and Sundays closed — plan a Bixby Knolls visit around a coffee stop on Atlantic Ave. either side of your appointment.

The center's approach is a good example of why bodywork and yoga therapy tend to overlap: chiropractic adjustment addresses joint and spinal mechanics, massage works the soft tissue around it, and a structured movement practice is what actually holds the improvement between visits. None of the three replaces the others.

## Assisted Stretch Therapy Near the Marina & East Long Beach

If your limitation is mobility rather than pain, assisted stretching is worth a look before you book another massage. **StretchLab** runs two Long Beach locations. The **Marina location** (6423 E Pacific Coast Hwy, Ste B, near Belmont Shore) is open extended weekday hours — Tuesday through Friday 7 a.m.–7 p.m., Monday 8 a.m.–7 p.m., and weekends 8 a.m.–1 p.m. — making it realistic to book a session before or after a shift. The **East Long Beach location** on Worsham Ave (3860 Worsham Ave, Ste 320, near CSULB) runs weekdays 8 a.m.–8 p.m. and weekends 9 a.m.–2 p.m.

A StretchLab session is one-on-one: a certified "Flexologist" moves your joints through a guided range of motion using proprietary tables and straps, rather than you stretching on your own on a mat. It's a genuinely different category from a deep-tissue massage — closer to physical therapy mobility work than a spa treatment — and it's worth trying if tight hips or shoulders are limiting your yoga practice, your run form, or just how you feel getting out of bed. Long Beach runners training for the October marathon or the Aloha Run later this month are a natural fit for a pre-race stretch session.

## What Happened to Downtown's Float Studio

One honest update, in keeping with how we run this directory: **FloatSpace**, the float-tank (sensory deprivation) studio that operated at 415 Pine Ave in Downtown Long Beach, is now closed according to its current Yelp listing. If float therapy — an hour alone in body-temperature, Epsom-salt water, floating in the dark — was on your list, the closest remaining option in Long Beach is the float therapy suite at **Pause Studio** (6242 Pacific Coast Hwy, Belmont Heights), which we covered in the cold-plunge guide alongside its contrast-therapy suites. We're not aware of a replacement studio in Downtown yet; if one opens, we'll add it here.

## Building Bodywork Into a Long Beach Recovery Week

The honest answer to "which one should I book" depends on what's actually bothering you. If you're dealing with joint stiffness, a chiropractic-and-massage visit at a place like Bixby Knolls Wellness Center addresses structure first. If the problem reads more like "I can't touch my toes" or "my hips feel locked after long runs," an assisted stretch session at StretchLab is the more targeted fix. And if you're recovering from a hard training block generally, sauna and cold plunge (our earlier guide) are the broader nervous-system reset. Most people who take recovery seriously eventually rotate through all three rather than picking one for life.

Bodywork also pairs naturally with a longer-term movement practice — massage and stretching loosen tissue, but a structured yoga therapy practice is what teaches the body to hold better patterns once the tightness is gone. If your Long Beach workplace already invests in employee wellbeing, on-site recovery and movement programming tend to work best together; My Yoga Network's [corporate wellness programs](https://myyoganetwork.com/corporate-wellness-programs) bring that structure to local teams, and individuals can start with [yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy) built around their own body and goals.
