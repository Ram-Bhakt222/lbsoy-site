Long Beach is a cardio town by geography — five miles of beach path, a flat bike grid, a bay you can paddle. What gets overlooked is how good the strength scene is. Between Signal Hill's industrial blocks, the PCH corridor, and a genuinely serious network of free outdoor equipment in the city's parks, you can train hard here for $0 to $200 a month depending on what you actually want.

Below: the gyms worth knowing, sorted by what they're actually good at. Every listing was verified open as of August 2026 — but call ahead, because gym hours change more than anything else in this directory.

## Boutique Strength & Circuit Gyms in Belmont Shore

If you want coaching and a room that knows your name, start on the east side.

**ICONIX Fitness** — 4101 E Olympic Plaza, near Recreation Park and the Belmont Shore edge. The pitch is volume: more than 130 classes a week across strength, cardio, and group formats, on top of a full equipment floor. It's the closest thing Long Beach has to a big-club class schedule inside a locally run building, and it's a five-minute walk from the beach path if you want to finish outside.

**ONIX Fitness** — 5010 2nd St, right on the Belmont Shore main drag. A smaller studio built around circuit training, personal training, and recovery, with coaches carrying personal-training certifications rather than a weekend badge. This is the pick if you've never touched a barbell and want someone watching your setup for the first six months. It's also the easiest to walk or bike to of anything on this list.

## Big-Box Gyms in Downtown and East Long Beach

**Club Studio** — 6310A E Pacific Coast Hwy, at Marina Pacifica. A hybrid model: a large cardio-and-strength floor with treadmills, bikes, rowers, and a full rack setup, plus separate boutique studios under the same membership. Good value if you'd otherwise buy a gym membership *and* a class pass.

**EōS Fitness** — two Long Beach locations, 75 Aquarium Way downtown on the waterfront and 5810 Downey Ave in North Long Beach. Standard big-box economics — long hours, a lot of equipment, low monthly cost. The Aquarium Way location is the practical choice if you work downtown and want to lift before walking the Shoreline path home.

## Hardcore Lifting: Signal Hill

**Iron Addicts Gym** — 2224A Cerritos Ave, Signal Hill, open 5 a.m. to 10 p.m. every day. This is the serious-lifter destination in greater Long Beach: heavy free weights, a no-frills floor, and a culture built around actually moving weight rather than posting about it. If your last three gyms didn't have enough plates, this is the answer. It is not the room to learn in — go to a coached studio first, then come here.

## CrossFit & Functional Strength

**CrossFit Modern** — 1180 N Studebaker Rd, Ste C, East Long Beach. Group strength-and-conditioning classes plus personal training, with programming that leans harder on the strength side than typical CrossFit.

**CrossFit Reality** — Signal Hill, serving greater Long Beach. Group classes, coaching, and HYROX-specific training for people chasing the hybrid race format that's taken over the fitness calendar.

**Molivate Gym** — 5401 E Village Rd, near El Dorado Park in East Long Beach. Trains members across competitive formats including CrossFit and powerlifting, which makes it a reasonable landing spot if you want to actually compete rather than just train.

## Free Strength Training: Long Beach's Outdoor Fitness Zones

This is the part almost nobody uses, and it's the best deal in the city. Long Beach Parks, Recreation and Marine maintains free outdoor gyms — real equipment, not just monkey bars — across nine parks. Their own framing is blunt: *avoid expensive gym memberships.*

- **Downtown Fitness Loop** — a 4.8-mile walking/running path bounded by Alamitos Ave, Shoreline Dr, Aquarium Rd, and Ocean Blvd, with distance markers, five active stations (pull-up and dip bars) and six passive stations (balance, stretch, squats). Pair it with the Shoreline path and you have a full session.
- **Bixby Fitness Loop** — near Junipero Ave and Ocean Blvd in **Alamitos Beach**, a marked walking path connecting six equipment stations. Steps from the daily free Yoga on the Bluff.
- **Houghton Park Fitness Loop** — 6301 Myrtle Ave, **North Long Beach**. A half-mile loop with bilingual instruction signs and QR codes linking to demo videos.
- **Lincoln Park** — 101 Pacific Ave, **Downtown**, dawn to dusk daily. ADA-accessible equipment covering all major muscle groups.
- **14th Street Park** — between Locust and Palmer Court. Leg press, chest press, vertical press, pull-downs, a cross-country ski machine, and a paved circuit path. Accessible for people with disabilities.
- **Orizaba Park** — Orizaba and Spaulding, **East Long Beach**. Equipment grouped by purpose: balance/chest/back, cardio and plyometrics, lower body with ADA-accessible pull-ups, and upper body/core.
- **Admiral Kidd Park Life Trail** — 2125 Santa Fe Ave, **Westside**. Paved paths with posted activities and stationary equipment, built on the idea that "play has no age."
- **Coolidge Park** — 352 E Neece St, with equipment near the playground so you can train while supervising kids.
- **Cesar E. Chavez Park** — six pieces of self-use equipment, three of them ADA accessible, north of 5th Street.
