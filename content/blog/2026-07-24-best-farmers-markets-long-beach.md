The single easiest habit for eating well in Long Beach costs nothing to start: show up to a farmers market. The city has a certified market almost every day of the week, spread across its neighborhoods, and mid-to-late summer is peak season — stone fruit, tomatoes, melons, corn, and berries are all at their best right now. A market run is also a genuinely healthy outing on its own: you walk, you're outside, you talk to the people who grew your food, and you come home with a fridge full of produce instead of packaged snacks. Here's the day-by-day guide to where to buy local and organic in Long Beach, plus how to stretch your dollars if you're on CalFresh.

## Tuesday & Saturday: Bixby Park in Alamitos Beach

The **Bixby Park** market (130 Cherry Ave., **Alamitos Beach**) runs twice a week and is the one with the best backdrop — the Pacific is right across Ocean Boulevard, kids are on the playground, and there's usually live music on a small stage. Tuesdays it runs **3–8 p.m.**; Saturdays it returns **9 a.m.–2 p.m.** Expect farm-fresh fruit and vegetables, gourmet specialty foods, and local makers selling jewelry, soaps, and candles, plus a strong prepared-food row — the empanadas and pupusas are local favorites. If you only hit one market this week and you're anywhere near the coast, make it this one. Time it right and you can pair a Saturday shop with the free 11 a.m. Yoga on the Bluff session a few steps away at the Bixby Park Annex.

## Wednesday: Marine Stadium by Belmont Shore

The **Marine Stadium** market (5255 Paoli Way), near **Belmont Shore**, is a traditional favorite that runs **3–7 p.m.** on the water at the historic rowing venue that hosted the 1932 Olympics. It's known for California-grown fruit and vegetables and stunning fresh-cut flowers, and over the years it has grown a deep artisanal bench — bread, honey, vinegar, coffee, and gourmet specialty foods. It's an easy afternoon-into-evening outing: shop, grab something to eat, and watch the light on the water.

## Thursday: Retro Row and Bixby Knolls

Thursday gives you two very different options. On **Retro Row**, the **Fourth Street Farmers Market** (4th Street & Cherry Ave.) is the city's newest, running **8 a.m.–noon** and leaning chef- and quality-driven. Vendors include **Hey Brother Baker** sourdough, **Long Beach Mushrooms**, **Stepladder Ranch & Creamery** (meats and cheeses), and **Black Sheep Farms** (dried beans and goods). Can't make the morning hours? You can pre-order a **Weiser Family Farms** produce box and pick it up at nearby **Alder & Sage** (366 Cherry Ave.) until 8 p.m. on Thursdays.

Up in **Bixby Knolls**, the market on Atlantic Avenue between E. 45th and 46th Streets runs **3–7 p.m.** in summer with a warm, neighborly feel — face painters and balloon artists for kids alongside great produce, Asian vegetables and melons, handmade soap, local pantry staples, fresh seafood, and Southern-style BBQ that draws a crowd on its own.

## Friday: Downtown Long Beach

The **Downtown Long Beach** market runs **10 a.m.–2 p.m.** near Fourth Street and the North Promenade, and it's the most bike- and transit-accessible of the bunch — it even offers free bicycle valet. It's known for excellent fruit (cherries, apricots, and nectarines in season, plus harder-to-find prickly pear and dragon fruit) as well as sustainable household goods and ready-to-eat food. If you work downtown, it's an easy walk from most of the East Village.

## Saturday: Wrigley on the Westside

Beyond Bixby Park, Saturday adds the **Wrigley Farmers Market** (behind Long Beach Beer Lab, 518 W. Willow St., on the **Westside**), which runs **9 a.m.–2 p.m.** under the shade of solar panels with its Shop Small Club. Alongside seasonal fruit and vegetables and artisanal bread, you'll find clothing, handmade soaps, artwork, jewelry, and food vendors — a community market with a neighborhood-maker bent.

## Sunday: Alamitos Bay and East Long Beach

Two Sunday markets round out the week. The **Alamitos Bay** market (205 N. Marina Dr., across from 2ND & PCH near **Naples**) runs **9 a.m.–2 p.m.** on the harbor and, fittingly for its location, is heavy on fresh seafood, Mediterranean treats, meats, jellies, and popular artisanal cheeses. Farther inland in **East Long Beach**, the smaller, more relaxed **Farmers Marketplace of Long Beach** at Clark & Spring (5000 E. Spring St., near the airport) runs **8 a.m.–1 p.m.** with produce, baked goods, roasted nuts, honey, cut flowers, and live music — the stroll-and-shop option when you want less of a crowd.

## Stretching Your Dollars: CalFresh and Market Match

Eating well in Long Beach shouldn't depend on your budget. Several of the city's markets — including the Harbor Area markets on **Thursday (Bixby Knolls)**, **Friday (Downtown)**, and **Sunday (Alamitos Bay)** — accept EBT, WIC, and CalFresh, and run the **Market Match** program. Spend $15 or more of your CalFresh benefit at the information booth and they'll match up to $15 in coupons that buy fresh fruits and vegetables, once per market day. That effectively doubles your produce budget — one of the best-value healthy-eating programs in the city.
