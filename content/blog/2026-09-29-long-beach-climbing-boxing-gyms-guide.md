Our [first strength guide](/blog/2026-08-13-best-gyms-strength-training-long-beach) covered the usual ways to build strength in Long Beach: boutique circuit studios in Belmont Shore, big-box gyms Downtown, the hardcore lifting floor in Signal Hill, CrossFit boxes, and the city's nine free outdoor Fitness Zones. This one covers the gyms where you get strong without really counting reps.

Climbing and boxing are both full-body strength training. Grip, pulling strength, core stability, footwork, and conditioning all get worked, and both come with a coached, social structure that keeps a lot of people showing up long after a solo gym membership would have lapsed. Long Beach has good options for both, clustered in Downtown, the East Village edge, and Signal Hill.

Every listing below had a current schedule or active listing as of late September 2026. Hours and pricing change often, so check before you go.

## Bouldering in Downtown Long Beach: Long Beach Rising

**Long Beach Rising** (205 E Anaheim St, next door to Trademark Brewing) is a 17,000-square-foot bouldering gym inside "The Packard," a 1926 landmark building that started life as an automobile showroom. Bouldering means climbing short walls over thick padded flooring without ropes or a partner. That makes it the easiest way into the sport: show up, put on rental shoes, and start on the easiest problems.

The gym runs daily intro classes for new climbers. Beyond the walls there are weights, training boards, two studio spaces with yoga and fitness classes (plus pole), and saunas. It's one of the few places in the city where you can boulder, lift, stretch, and sit in a sauna under one membership. Reviewers regularly describe it as friendlier and more intimate than the big chain gyms. Short-term passes and full memberships are both available, and the gym hosts social nights with film screenings and DJs.

## Ropes and Auto-Belays in Signal Hill: Hangar 18

**Hangar 18 – Long Beach** (2599 E Willow St, **Signal Hill**) has about 12,000 square feet of walls for top-rope climbing, auto-belays, and bouldering. Auto-belays are mechanical rope systems that let you climb tall walls alone, which makes them good for interval-style endurance sessions. First-timers take an intro climbing class before getting wall access. After that, the training area and yoga room are open to you.

The gym has been operating since 1998 and sells monthly and annual memberships for individuals and families. It's also close to Signal Hill's hilltop parks, so you can climb indoors and then walk the Panorama Promenade to cool down. That's a strong half-day in one small neighborhood.

## The Biggest Wall Near Long Beach Airport: Sender One

**Sender One** (4949 Lakewood Blvd, just over the city line in Lakewood near Long Beach Airport) is the largest of the three at 29,000 square feet. It has top-rope and bouldering walls up to 50 feet, a fitness area with treadmills and weights, and stretching, yoga, and meditation classes. **Sender City** is a separate, colorful climbing zone built for kids, so it's the most family-friendly option on this list.

Hours are the longest too: 6 a.m.–10 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m.–6 p.m. on weekends, which works if you want to climb before work. Day passes and memberships are available, and the gym books corporate team-building sessions.

## Boxing & Kickboxing on 4th Street: Hard Worx Fitness

**Hard Worx Fitness – 4th Street** (1936 E 4th St, on the **Retro Row** end of the 4th Street Corridor) is a hybrid gym. It offers boxing, kickboxing, and HIIT classes alongside Muay Thai, MMA technique, and no-gi Brazilian jiu-jitsu, and it has an open gym floor with racks, dumbbells, kettlebells, and cardio machines. On a typical weekday the schedule runs from a noon kickboxing class through evening cardio boxing and BJJ, so a lunch or after-work session is easy to fit in. It holds a 4.9 rating across more than 500 ClassPass reviews.

If you're new, start with a Cardio Boxing or Kickboxing Worx class. They're built for conditioning, not sparring, and you'll learn the basic stance and punches before anything with contact.

## Old-School Boxing in Signal Hill: Hill Street Boxing Gym

**Hill Street Boxing Gym** (1201 E Hill St, **Signal Hill**) is the more traditional counterpoint. It's a Latinx-owned boxing gym with evening Open Boxing sessions and a stated mission of turning fitness into a lasting habit rather than a January sprint. It holds a 4.7 rating across 100+ ClassPass reviews. Bag work, footwork, and conditioning rounds are about as efficient as a one-hour workout gets. Like Hangar 18, it's a short drive from the Signal Hill trail loops.

## Gear Up in Los Altos: Sports Basement

Once you're hooked, **Sports Basement** (2100 N Bellflower Blvd, **Los Altos**, in the former Sears building) carries climbing shoes, harnesses, chalk, and crash pads, and rents gear by the day, weekend, week, or month. That's useful before you commit to buying your own shoes. It's open 10 a.m.–8 p.m. on weekdays and 9 a.m.–7 p.m. on weekends. The second floor has five free indoor pickleball courts (sign a waiver first), which count as a solid low-cost workout on their own.

## Training Smart: Hands, Shoulders, and Recovery

Climbing and boxing are both hard on the same few body parts: fingers and forearms, shoulders, and wrists. Most people who quit don't lose motivation. They get a sore finger pulley or an angry shoulder and never come back. A few habits help:

- Warm up with easy climbing or shadowboxing before anything hard.
- Keep your first month to two or three sessions a week so tendons can adapt, since they adapt more slowly than muscle.
- Balance all the pulling and punching with mobility work.

This is where yoga fits in. A community yoga class at one of these gyms is a good way to open the hips and shoulders after a session. If a joint is already complaining, that calls for a different tool. A one-on-one [yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy) assessment looks at the specific movement pattern that's breaking down, and it's clinical work, not a drop-in class.
