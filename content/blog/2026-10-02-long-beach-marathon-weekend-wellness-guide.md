Long Beach is about to have its busiest movement weekend of the year. The 2XU Long Beach Marathon takes over the waterfront on October 10-11, 2026, and the organizers expect more than 28,000 participants across the 5K, half marathon and full marathon. Every distance is already sold out, so most readers will meet this weekend as neighbors, spectators or volunteers rather than runners. That is a fine way to do it. The route is one of the most scenic in Southern California, and the days around it are full of low-key, healthy things to do.

This guide covers what is confirmed, how to enjoy the weekend on foot, and where to wind down afterward.

## The 2026 Long Beach Marathon Schedule (Downtown and the Waterfront)

Here is the confirmed schedule from the official race site.

**Race expo and packet pickup** is at the Long Beach Convention Center, Hall C (400 E Seaside Way). It runs Friday, October 9 from 1 to 7 p.m. and Saturday, October 10 from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. Marathon and half marathon participants must collect bibs at the expo. The 5K can be picked up Friday or on race morning.

**The 5K** starts Saturday, October 10 at 6:50 a.m. from the Aquarium of the Pacific, with early bib pickup on site from 5:15 to 6:30 a.m. It is the friendliest distance for first-timers and walkers, and the course has a 1.5-hour time limit.

**The marathon** starts Sunday, October 11 at 5:30 a.m. at Shoreline Drive and Shoreline Village Drive. **The half marathon** starts at 6:30 a.m. from the same spot. The course winds past the Queen Mary, Shoreline Village, the beach path, Belmont Shore and Cal State Long Beach, and it is mostly flat. The half is the postcard one, with roughly 90 percent of it in view of the Pacific.

**The Finish Line Festival** runs Sunday from 7 a.m. to 1 p.m. at Marina Green, with food trucks, a team village and photo spots.

The race site says parking and road-closure details were still to come when we checked, so look at the official page before you drive downtown. Plan on walking, biking or taking transit if you can.

## Cheering From Belmont Shore and the Beach Path

You do not need a bib to enjoy the course. The stretches along the beach path and through Belmont Shore are easy to reach on foot, and spectating is its own kind of exercise. Walk a mile to a good viewing spot, cheer for an hour, then walk somewhere else and do it again.

If you want a quiet walk before the crowds, go early on Saturday. The shoreline between the Aquarium and Marina Green is flat and open, and it is the same ground the 5K covers a few hours later. Our [guide to Long Beach walking paths](/blog/2026-08-04-best-hikes-bluff-beach-walking-paths-long-beach) has longer routes if you want to keep going.

Volunteers keep the whole event running. The organizers recruit for shifts at water stations and the finish area, and you can sign up at runlongbeach.com/volunteers.

## Before the Race: A Wetlands Weekend in Long Beach

The weekend before marathon weekend is a good counterweight to all that pavement. The Los Cerritos Wetlands Land Trust has a guided nature walk on Saturday, October 3, 8 to 10 a.m., at Gum Grove Park on the Seal Beach side of the wetlands complex. RSVP is required. A habitat restoration morning with the Aquarium of the Pacific follows the same day from 10:30 a.m. to 12:30 p.m. It is free, and registration is required. On Sunday, October 4 at 9:30 a.m., the land trust runs a kayak tour from Mother's Beach (5733 Appian Way) for a $40 donation, ages 12 and up.

These details come from the organizations' own listings, so check each page for any last-minute changes. For the full story on the wetlands, see our [Long Beach wetland walks guide](/blog/2026-09-28-long-beach-wetland-lagoon-walks-guide).

## Marathon Weekend Recovery in East Village and Belmont Shore

Whether you ran 26.2 miles or walked 15,000 steps cheering, your body will want a plan for Monday. The basics are boring and they work: water, a real meal, a short walk, and some gentle stretching rather than a hard second workout.

Long Beach already has what you need for the rest. Our guides to [massage and bodywork](/blog/2026-09-08-long-beach-massage-bodywork-recovery-guide), [cold plunge and sauna recovery](/blog/2026-06-29-cold-plunge-sauna-recovery-long-beach) and [juice bars and smoothies](/blog/2026-06-16-best-juice-bars-smoothies-acai-long-beach) cover the options from East Village to Belmont Shore. Call ahead about hours, and book early if you can, because a lot of runners will have the same idea.

For runners with a recurring pain that keeps coming back after long efforts, such as a tight low back or hips, that is a conversation for a credentialed [yoga therapist](/yoga-therapy) rather than a foam roller. And for Long Beach employers who watched a few hundred staff sign up for a race and wondered how to support that energy all year, [corporate wellness programming](/corporate-wellness) and the [My Yoga Network corporate wellness network](https://myyoganetwork.com/corporate-wellness-programs) are a natural next step.

## Still Running Next Year?

Early registration for the 2027 race is open on the official site, with introductory pricing that starts at $66 and rises through later release phases. Registration fees are non-refundable, so decide before you click. If you want a gentler way into running in the meantime, Long Beach has plenty of run clubs and weekly walks. Start with our [run and walk clubs guide](/blog/2026-06-18-best-run-walk-clubs-long-beach).

## A Simple Plan for the Weekend

Friday, take a walk along the water and skip the expo crowd unless you need your bib. Saturday, pick one thing: the wetlands walk, the restoration morning or the 5K. Sunday, get up early or sleep in, but plan your route before you leave so road closures do not surprise you. Monday, walk, stretch and eat well.
