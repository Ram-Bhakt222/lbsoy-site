Long Beach's best-known run clubs cluster around Belmont Shore and the beach path — and we covered those first. But some of the city's most durable, community-built running groups started somewhere else entirely: a Bixby Knolls parking lot, a Signal Hill Home Depot lot, and a bluff-top meeting spot that's been welcoming LGBTQ+ runners and allies for years. This update fills in the map with the neighborhood crews that don't always show up in a first search.

## Bixby Knolls Dawn Joggers: The Group a Prowler Accidentally Built

The origin story is worth knowing before you show up. In 2016, Bixby Knolls resident Marcelle Epley was followed for half a mile by a driver during her regular 5 a.m. run — one of several similar reports from women in the neighborhood that year. Rather than stop running, she posted on Facebook and Nextdoor asking if anyone wanted to run together. Over 70 people responded. The **Bixby Knolls Dawn Joggers** were born from five or six people meeting at the Jamba Juice on Atlantic and Carson, and the group has since grown into one of the neighborhood's largest standing fitness communities, with an eastside offshoot as well.

The habit hasn't changed: the group meets every weekday, rain or shine, in front of Jamba Juice at Atlantic Avenue and Carson Street (4085 Atlantic Ave.), leaving sharp at 5:15 a.m. — people start arriving around 5:05. Monday, Wednesday, and Friday are led runs; Tuesday and Thursday are led walks, which makes the group genuinely beginner-friendly despite its pace-runner reputation. Routes run 3–4 miles and change session to session, printed and handed out at the meetup. It's free, no sign-up required, and the group doesn't meet on holidays.

## Signal Hill: A Monday Evening Habit and a Wednesday Morning One

Signal Hill's running scene is smaller but consistent. A weekly group run meets Mondays at 6:30 p.m. at the Home Depot lot (2450 Cherry Ave.), and a separate Wednesday 6 a.m. session gathers at Chittick Field. Both take advantage of Signal Hill's actual elevation — a rarity in a city that otherwise reads pancake-flat — with the **Signal Hill Trail** at Hilltop Park nearby for anyone who wants to extend a run into an incline finish with harbor and Catalina views.

## Shoreline Frontrunners of Long Beach: An Inclusive Crew on the Bluff

**Shoreline Frontrunners of Long Beach** is part of the international Frontrunners network, built around LGBTQ+ runners, walkers, and allies, and it's been a fixture on the Alamitos Beach bluff for years. The group runs three times a week: Tuesdays and Thursdays at 6:30 p.m. (6 p.m. in winter) at the bluff at Redondo Avenue and Ocean Boulevard, covering 3–6 miles along the beach path and back, plus a more social Saturday run-and-walk at 9 a.m. from the same starting point. Tuesday runs occasionally shift to Signal Hill, meeting at the same Home Depot lot. All paces and abilities are welcome, and the club is explicit that walkers are as much a part of the group as fast runners — a tone that carries through its marathon-training program and its annual Pride Run.

## Where the Established Clubs Still Fit In

None of this replaces Long Beach's bigger-name groups — Long Beach Run Club's Saturday miles from Coronado and Ocean, Run Club Girls' two-pace format, or the Lululemon-hosted runs near Belmont Shore — which we covered in detail in our [first Long Beach run and walk club guide](/blog/2026-06-18-best-run-walk-clubs-long-beach). Think of this as the second layer: the neighborhood-specific groups that formed organically, stayed small on purpose, or serve a community that a general-interest club doesn't always reach.

## Coming Up: The Aloha Run, September 20

If you want a race to train toward with any of these groups, the **Aloha Run Long Beach** returns Sunday, September 20, 2026, at Shoreline Aquatic Park (200 Aquarium Way), with 5K and 10K run/walk options along the harbor, beach, and bay. Registration and packet pickup open at 6:30 a.m., the 10K starts at 7:30 a.m., and the 5K starts at 7:45 a.m.; both include a finisher medal, an Aloha Run beach T-shirt, chip timing, and a post-race festival with live music. It's a realistic four-week goal from any of the groups above, and several Long Beach clubs organize informal team turnouts.

## How to Show Up

Pick one group and go once before judging it. Arrive 5–10 minutes early, introduce yourself, and don't worry about pace on your first visit — every club listed here, from the Dawn Joggers to the Frontrunners, structures itself around walkers and slower runners as a permanent feature, not an afterthought. Long Beach's running culture is genuinely more welcoming than its Strava leaderboard suggests.
