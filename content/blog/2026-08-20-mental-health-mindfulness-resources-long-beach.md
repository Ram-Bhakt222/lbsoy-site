## Long Beach Has More Mental Health Infrastructure Than Most Locals Realize

If you're searching for support in **Long Beach** — a meditation practice to start, a therapist who takes your insurance, or a crisis line for tonight — the options are wider than a quick Google search usually surfaces. This guide organizes what's actually here: donation-based meditation communities in **Alamitos Beach** and **Belmont Heights**, low-cost and sliding-scale counseling across the city, and the crisis resources worth saving in your phone before you need them.

This is a directory, not a diagnosis. Nothing here replaces a conversation with a licensed provider, and if you're in crisis right now, skip to the [Crisis Resources](#crisis-resources-in-long-beach) section below and use one of those lines — they're free, confidential, and staffed by trained people.

## Meditation & Mindfulness Communities

Long Beach's meditation scene is small but genuinely rooted, with several groups that have been running for years rather than months.

**Long Beach Meditation** (5100 E. The Toledo, Alamitos Beach) is the city's longest-standing meditation sangha — a nonprofit that's been serving Long Beach and the surrounding area for over three decades. Sessions are donation-based and meet at Bay Shore Community Church, a space the organization describes as ADA accessible, LGBTQ+ welcoming, and outfitted with gender-neutral restrooms. The weekly schedule includes sits Monday and Wednesday evenings (7–8:15 p.m.), a shorter Friday session (4:30–5:15 p.m.), and Sunday afternoon (3–4:30 p.m.), plus periodic Mindfulness Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) course offerings and an online option via Zoom for anyone who can't make it in person. It's inspired by Buddhist teaching but explicitly welcomes people of any or no religious background.

**InsightLA — Long Beach** holds its local sits at the Sacred Roots Healing Center on E. Broadway, an offshoot of the larger InsightLA meditation organization based in Los Angeles. It's a good option if you've sampled meditation apps and want an in-person teacher and community instead.

**Atlantic Meditation** (4320 Atlantic Ave.) runs a more structured, studio-style offering — in-person or by Zoom, with a low-cost introductory session and hours six days a week (closed Sundays). It leans toward stress, overthinking, and sleep as the practical entry points, which makes it approachable if "meditation" has felt too abstract or spiritual in the past.

If sitting still isn't your style, **Yogalution Movement & Wellness** on the **Alamitos Beach** bluff — home of the free daily Yoga on the Bluff — also folds meditation and breathwork into its donation-based offerings, and a handful of the city's yoga studios covered in our [yoga and movement studios guide](/blog/2026-08-03-best-yoga-movement-studios-long-beach) run sound baths and restorative sessions built around mindfulness rather than physical intensity.

## Finding a Therapist in Long Beach

Long Beach has a deep bench of licensed therapists, and the honest starting point is a directory rather than a single "best" name — fit matters more than ranking. **Zencare** and **TherapyDen** let you filter Long Beach providers by specialty, insurance, and sliding-scale availability. **SonderMind** lists a large roster of Long Beach-area therapists currently accepting new clients. Private-pay rates locally run roughly $150–$250 a session, which is why the sliding-scale and Medi-Cal clinics below matter for a lot of residents.

For lower-cost and insurance-based options, the City of Long Beach Health Department publishes a **Mental Health Resource Guide** listing local providers by neighborhood, payment type, and language. A few worth knowing by name: **Memorial Counseling Associates** (4525 E. Atherton St.) is a multi-disciplinary practice — psychiatrists, psychologists, and licensed therapists under one roof — that accepts most private insurance plus Medicare and Medi-Cal with an HMO plan. **Long Beach Center for Psychotherapy** (5855 E. Naples Plaza) accepts most PPO plans, Medicare, and cash pay for individual and family counseling. **Jewish Family & Children's Service** (3801 E. Willow St.) offers sliding-scale counseling for adults and children and is open to the whole community regardless of faith background. **The LGBTQ Center Long Beach** (2017 E. 4th St., **East Village**) offers sliding-scale counseling for adults, youth, and families with no insurance required. And **Mental Health America of Los Angeles** (200 Pine Ave., **Downtown**) runs general adult and child services plus dedicated postpartum-depression support.

If cost is the main barrier, start with the **LA County Department of Mental Health Long Beach office** (1975 N. Long Beach Blvd., or the countywide Access Line at 800-854-7771) — no referral is needed, and intake staff can route you to a Medi-Cal-accepting provider based on need and language.

## Support Groups & Community-Based Help

Not every kind of support needs a clinical setting. **NAMI Long Beach/Whittier** runs free peer-led groups — NAMI Connection for people living with a mental health condition, and a Family Support Group for relatives and caregivers; reach the chapter through the national NAMI HelpLine (1-800-950-6264) for current times. **New Hope Grief Support Community** (3505 Long Beach Blvd.) runs structured 8-week grief groups for children and adults, in person and by Zoom, with scholarships available. **Alzheimer's Los Angeles** offers free caregiver support groups county-wide, including Long Beach sessions. And **Moms & Babies**, through Miller Children's Hospital, hosts a free weekly Zoom group for new mothers (Tuesdays, 10 a.m.–noon) alongside prenatal and postpartum counseling referrals.

## Crisis Resources in Long Beach

Save these before you need them.

**988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline** — call or text 988, available 24/7. Free, confidential, and the front door to a nationwide network of local crisis counselors.

**Crisis Text Line** — text "LA" to 741741, 24/7, for anyone who'd rather text than talk.

**LA County DMH Access Line** — 800-854-7771, 24/7. Screening, crisis counseling, and mobile field-response teams for LA County, including Long Beach.

**NAMI HelpLine** — 800-950-6264 (info@nami.org), Monday–Friday, 10 a.m.–6 p.m. Peer support and referrals, not a crisis line, but a good next call once the immediate moment has passed.

**Trevor Project Lifeline** — 800-488-7386 (call), or text "START" to 678-678, for LGBTQ youth in crisis.

**Veterans Crisis Line** — dial 988, then press 1, or text 838255.

**Hacienda of Hope** (Project Return, 2241 W. Williams St.) — a free, short-term residential respite alternative to an ER visit for people in a stressful crisis who need a safe place to stay for a few days.

None of these require insurance, a referral, or proof of anything. They exist for exactly the moment you'd call them.

## A Note on How We Think About This

We're a **yoga therapy** and corporate wellness company, and it would be easy to overstate what a meditation class or a yoga session can do for a mental health condition. We won't. Meditation and movement can be genuinely useful tools for everyday stress and can complement clinical care — but they are not a substitute for therapy, medication, or crisis support when those are what's actually needed. If you're working with a **yoga therapist** on anxiety or stress as part of a broader care plan, that's a legitimate adjunct to treatment; it is not the treatment itself.
