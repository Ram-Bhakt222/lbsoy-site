Summer gets the postcards, but locals know the truth: fall is when Long Beach is actually best to live in. The crowds thin out, the marine layer lifts earlier, the water is still warm enough to swim without a wetsuit, and the air finally drops into the range where you can run at noon without regretting it. October in Long Beach averages a high near 75°F with nights around 62°F — close to ideal training weather — and the ocean holds around 64.8°F, down from a September peak near 69°F. That combination is rare, and it lasts about ten weeks.

Here's how to use it.

## Fall Is Long Beach's Real Running Season

The single biggest date on the local calendar is **2XU Long Beach Marathon weekend, October 10–11**. The marathon itself runs Sunday, October 11 with a 5:30 a.m. start on a flat, USATF-certified, Boston-qualifying course past the Queen Mary and out through Belmont Shore. The half marathon, 5K, and bike tour fill out the weekend, with the race expo and packet pickup at the Long Beach Convention Center Hall C on Friday, October 9 (1–7 p.m.) and Saturday, October 10 (10 a.m.–4 p.m.). Registration for 2026 sold out with a waitlist, so check runlongbeach.com before you plan on entering — but the expo is open regardless, and spectating the course along Ocean Boulevard is free.

Even if you're not racing, the taper-season energy is worth borrowing. Two free, no-signup walking groups run all fall out of **Belmont Shore**: **Tour the Shore** meets every Saturday at 7:30 a.m. at 2nd Street and La Verne for a 2–3 mile loop to a different point of interest each week (participants get discounts at Chapter II Coffee), and **WOW — Walk On Wednesdays** gathers at 7:15 a.m. in front of Chase Bank and leaves promptly at 7:30 for a brisk 3-mile hour, rotating through twelve routes that all touch water: the ocean, the bay, the canals, Marine Stadium, the Colorado Lagoon. Both welcome walkers, skaters, and wheelchair users.

## Cooler Air Means the Trails Open Back Up in East Long Beach

The **El Dorado Nature Center** (7550 E. Spring St.) is the closest thing Long Beach has to a real forest walk, and it's dramatically better in fall than in August. Trails are open Tuesday–Sunday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m., with no entry after 4:30 p.m.; the museum runs 8:30 a.m.–4 p.m. There's a quarter-mile paved loop straight out of the visitor center that handles strollers and wheelchairs, plus two one-mile dirt trails that run deeper into the sanctuary over stream bridges and out to the South Lake. Walking or biking in is free — only cars pay. Go early, when the light is low and the sycamores are turning.

For elevation, **Hilltop Park** in Signal Hill and the bluff-top loop above **Alamitos Beach** both get their best visibility of the year in fall, when the haze clears and Catalina shows up on the horizon.

## The Water Is Still Warm — Use It Before It Isn't

September holds the warmest ocean temperatures of the entire year here, and October only drops a few degrees. That makes early fall the best swimming, paddling, and open-water window Long Beach gets, not the end of one.

It's also cleanup season. **Coastal Cleanup Day is Saturday, September 19, 2026**, with 60-plus sites across L.A. County spanning beaches, the L.A. River, parks, trails, and underwater dive sites; Heal the Bay coordinates and registration is already open. If you miss it, the **Surfrider Long Beach chapter** runs a cleanup on the **first Saturday of every month at Belmont Pier** (Ocean Blvd. and 39th Place), 10 a.m.–12 p.m. Gloves, bags, and pickers are provided, parking passes come from the tent, and volunteers 14 and under need an adult. It's two hours of low-intensity outdoor movement that happens to be useful — the easiest wellness habit in the city to justify.

## Fall Festivals Worth Walking To

Long Beach's fall event calendar leans food-and-festival, but most of it is walkable and outdoors, which is the whole point.

- **Belmont Shore Car Show** — Sunday, September 13, 9 a.m.–3 p.m. One of the West Coast's largest, free for spectators, and effectively a two-mile walk down a closed 2nd Street.
- **Oktoberfest at Steelcraft Long Beach** — September 25–27, with an official bar crawl Saturday, September 26 from 4 p.m.
- **Fall Into Autumn Arts, Crafts & Food Market Fest** — October 2–4, 10 a.m.–6 p.m. at Shoreline Aquatic Park, Downtown. Free admission, waterfront setting, and an easy add-on to a Shoreline Pedestrian Bikepath ride.
- **Trick-or-Treat on 2nd Street** — the Belmont Shore Business Association's long-running Halloween evening on 2nd Street, with participating storefronts marked by jack-o'-lantern signs.

Fall is also when the farmers markets get good. Long Beach runs a certified market nearly every day — Bixby Park Tuesdays 3–8 p.m. and Saturdays 9 a.m.–2 p.m., Downtown Fridays 10 a.m.–2 p.m. at City Place, Alamitos Bay Marina Sundays 9 a.m.–2 p.m. — and the autumn table shifts to squash, apples, pomegranates, persimmons, and citrus coming back on.

## Yoga on the Bluff Gets Its Best Season

**Yoga on the Bluff** — the free daily 11 a.m. yoga class on the grass at Ocean Boulevard and Junipero Avenue, run by Yogalution Movement & Wellness — is a year-round Long Beach institution, but fall is when it peaks. The seasonal 6 p.m. weekday sunset sessions run April through October, and October sunsets over the Pacific from the bluff are the reason people move here. Classes are donation-based and never required, and the only cancellations are for rain or wet ground. Bring a mat or practice on the grass.

## Making the Seasonal Reset Stick

Fall is the honest reset month — school is back, the calendar tightens, and most people's summer routine quietly collapses. The move is to attach one new habit to something already scheduled: the Saturday walking group, the first-Saturday cleanup, the 11 a.m. bluff session on your work-from-home days.

If what's actually in the way is a health condition — persistent low back pain, an old shoulder, hypertension, anxiety that makes group settings hard — a general fitness class isn't the right first step. That's the territory of [yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy), where a credentialed yoga therapist builds a protocol around your specific condition rather than a room average.
