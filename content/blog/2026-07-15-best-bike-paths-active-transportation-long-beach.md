Long Beach was one of the first cities in the country to brand itself "the most bike-friendly city in America," and the infrastructure mostly backs it up. Four major off-road bike paths, a flat coastline, protected canals, and a downtown bike hub with showers make this one of the easiest places in Southern California to swap a short car trip for a ride. Whether you want a car-free morning loop, a long river route, or just a beach cruise with the family, here's where to ride — and where to grab a bike if you don't own one.

## The Shoreline Pedestrian Bikepath — Downtown to Belmont Shore

The **Shoreline Pedestrian Bikepath** (locals just call it "the beach path") is the one everyone starts with. It runs about 4.5 miles along the Pacific from **Shoreline Village** in Downtown Long Beach to the edge of **Belmont Shore**, and the core three-mile stretch from Shoreline Village to Alamitos Bay is a 17-foot-wide ribbon of pavement right on the sand. It's flat, wide, separated from car traffic, and open to all skill levels, with views of the beach, the Downtown skyline, and Catalina on a clear day. Because it's dead flat and fully paved, it's the friendliest ride in the city for kids, beginners, and anyone easing back into movement. Go early — by mid-afternoon in summer it fills with walkers, skaters, and rental surreys.

## The LA River and San Gabriel River Trails — Long Beach's Long Routes

When you want distance, Long Beach sits at the mouth of two river bikeways. The **Los Angeles River Bike Path** is a nearly 30-mile bikeway along the east side of the river; the Long Beach section is a roughly 4-mile stretch with the river on one side and green space on the other, connecting Downtown north toward the wider regional trail. On the east side of town, the **San Gabriel River Bike Trail** runs about 28 miles — one of the longest continuous off-street rides in the L.A. basin — passing through **El Dorado Regional Park** in **East Long Beach** and continuing down to **Alamitos Bay Marina**. Both are the routes to reach for when you want a real training ride or a car-free way to cover ground, and both connect back to the coast so you can loop the shoreline on the return.

## El Dorado and Heartwell — East Long Beach's Park Paths

Not every ride needs to be an epic. Inside **El Dorado Regional Park**, the **El Dorado Bike Path** is a roughly 4-mile paved loop through 450 acres of lakes, meadow, and woodland — shaded, low-traffic, and ideal for a relaxed family spin. A little further west, the **Heartwell Bike Path** threads about 2.5 miles through **Heartwell Park** along Carson Street, paralleling a fitness course and playgrounds. These interior park paths are the ones to choose on a hot day or when you're riding with young kids who aren't ready for the open coastline yet.

## Naples Island and Alamitos Bay — Riding the Canals

For a slower, scenic ride, the **Naples Island** canals and **Alamitos Bay** waterfront are hard to beat. The streets around the canals are calm and largely residential, so you can pedal past the little bridges and waterfront homes at a wander. If you'd rather be on the water than beside it, **Long Beach Waterbikes** at Alamitos Bay Landing runs reservation-only floating-bike rentals, including nighttime "glow rides" through the bay and canals — a genuinely memorable way to log some easy cardio after dark.

## Where to Rent or Grab a Bike in Long Beach

You don't need to own a bike to ride here. The **GoActiveLB Hub** at 223 E. 1st Street in **Downtown Long Beach** (open weekdays 9 a.m.–5 p.m., run by Pedal Movement) offers rentals, repairs, maps, retail, and even showers — a full-service base for a car-free day. It's also the anchor for **Long Beach Bike Share**, whose blue eight-gear bikes come with a basket, lights, and GPS and can be picked up and dropped at any designated hub around the city. Down at the water, **Wheel Fun Rentals** (429 Shoreline Village Dr.) rents cruisers, four-person surreys, and kid trailers steps from the beach path. If you're buying or need a tune-up, well-regarded local shops include **The Bicycle Stand** (2740 E. Broadway) in **Belmont Heights**, which refurbishes classic bikes; **Jax Bicycle Center** (3000 N. Bellflower Blvd.) for full retail and service; and **Propel Electric Bikes** (100 E. Broadway) for pedal-assist e-bikes if the hills of **Signal Hill** are your excuse for skipping a ride.

## Why Cycling Is the Easiest Health Habit in Long Beach

Riding a bike is one of the rare forms of exercise that doesn't feel like exercise — it's transportation, recreation, and low-impact cardio at once. For anyone whose knees or back can't take running, a flat coastal path is a gentle way to build cardiovascular fitness without pounding the joints. Long Beach's geography makes it unusually doable: most of the city is flat, the coastline is car-free, and errands to Belmont Shore or Downtown are genuinely rideable. If you're using cycling to work back from an injury or a chronic condition, it pairs well with more structured support — a **[yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy)** program can address the mobility and stability limits that a bike ride alone won't.
