Our first budget guide covered the big free staples: outdoor Fitness Zones, daily bluff yoga, sliding-scale care and Market Match. This second pass goes further into the free side of Long Beach, with things that are easy to overlook because they do not look like "wellness" at first glance. A pickleball court is cardio. A historic garden walk is a gentle mobility session. A library card is a ticket to a full day on a state-park trail. None of the entries below asks for a membership.

Everything here was checked against a city page, an official site, or a local listing in October 2026. Where a price or time was not stated, we say so rather than guess. Schedules shift, so confirm with each organizer before you go.

## Free Pickleball Courts Across Long Beach, From Belmont Shore to North Long Beach

Pickleball is one of the cheapest ways to get a real workout in the city. The City of Long Beach lists a network of free public courts that are first-come, first-served, with no reservations. Shared-use signage applies, so check the posted rules at each park.

The list on the city's pickleball page includes:

- **Bayshore Park** (5415 E. Ocean Blvd., near **Belmont Shore**): one dedicated court plus two dual-striped courts.
- **Junipero Beach** (2100 E. Ocean Blvd., **Alamitos Beach**): two dedicated courts on a repurposed half basketball court, a few steps from the sand.
- **Marina Vista Park** (Colorado St. and Santiago, **Belmont Heights**): four dedicated courts and four dual-striped courts.
- **Somerset Park** (1500 E. Carson St.): eight courts striped across two tennis courts.
- **Veterans Park** (101 E. 28th St.): eight courts striped on a tennis court, plus four dedicated courts.
- **DeForest Park** (6255 DeForest Ave., **North Long Beach**): eight dedicated courts.
- **Silverado Park** (1545 W. 31st St., **Westside**): four dedicated courts.
- **El Dorado Park West** (2800 N. Studebaker Rd.): three courts on a shared sports court.

If you want coaching, drop-in play at the city's tennis centers costs $5 per person for up to three hours, per the same page. That is a low-cost way to get a few games in before you head to a free court.

Indoors, **Sports Basement** (2100 N. Bellflower Blvd., **Los Altos**) has been reported to run first-come pickleball courts in its Flight Deck space, but we could not confirm current hours from the store's own page, so call ahead.

## Free Historic Ranchos: A Gentle Walk at Rancho Los Cerritos and Rancho Los Alamitos

Two of the oldest sites in the city offer free admission, and both reward a slow walk through gardens and grounds.

**Rancho Los Cerritos** (4600 Virginia Rd., **Bixby Knolls**) is free during public hours, and parking is free too; donations are welcome. Public hours are Wednesday through Friday and Sunday 1 to 5 p.m., and Saturday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. It is closed Monday and Tuesday. Reservations are only needed for groups of eight or more. The site's October calendar lists a guided bird walk on the second Thursday of each month (8 to 9:30 a.m.), so the next one should fall on Thursday, Nov. 12. A free Community Ofrenda altar runs in front of the Visitor Center from Oct. 14 through Nov. 8. The listing does not state prices for individual events, so check each event page.

**Rancho Los Alamitos** (6400 E. Bixby Hill Rd., **Alamitos Heights**) has also been listed by local outlets as free, with free parking and informal guided tours, Wednesday through Sunday 1 to 5 p.m. We could not load the ranch's own site, so treat the hours as a starting point and confirm before you drive over.

Why include them in a wellness guide? A garden loop is low-impact movement, shade, and quiet. For many older adults or anyone returning from an injury, a flat grounds walk is a useful first step before moving up to longer trails.

## Your Library Card Gets You Into State Parks

The **Long Beach Public Library** lets cardholders check out a **California State Parks Pass**. According to the library's page, the loan period is three weeks, with one renewal if nobody has placed a hold. You can place a hold through the catalog or ask staff at any branch's front desk. Held passes are kept for three open days, and you can borrow one pass at a time. Return it to a circulation desk at any branch, not the book drop. The page does not say which branches stock passes, so check the catalog first.

A pass is worth planning around. Pair it with a weekend hike somewhere outside the city, then come home and do the easy, flat loops we cover in our [Long Beach hikes and bluff walking guide](/blog/2026-08-04-best-hikes-bluff-beach-walking-paths-long-beach). The library also hosts free access to several digital resources with a card, which is a reminder that the most useful free "wellness program" in town may be one you already own.

## A Monthly Outdoor Sound Bath at Carroll Park South

**Community Sound Healing for Everyone, Long Beach** meets outdoors at **Carroll Park South** (2300 Carroll Park South), according to its Meetup page. The group describes its sessions as blending sound, breathing techniques, and group meditation, and the listing says no registration is required. The next sessions on the page are Saturday, Nov. 14, at 10:30 a.m., and Dec. 12. The page does not state a price, so ask the organizer if you plan to bring a group. Local listings describe it as a second-Saturday gathering. Bring a mat or blanket, since this is a park lawn, not a studio.

A few free recurring options are still running through the fall, including the Saturday **Tour the Shore** walk in **Belmont Shore** (7:30 a.m.), Surfrider's first-Saturday cleanup at **Belmont Pier** (next on Nov. 7), and the trails at the **El Dorado Nature Center**, where entry on foot or by bike is free. ## When "Free" Is Not Enough

Free activities are excellent for building the habit of moving. They are not a substitute for individualized care if you are managing chronic pain, recovering from surgery, or working around a heart or breathing condition. In those cases, start with an assessment from a credentialed professional. Our [yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy) page explains how a one-on-one approach works.
