Long Beach markets its water sports around kayak rentals and kitesurfing lessons — and [we've covered that side of the city before](/blog/2026-06-21-surf-swim-paddle-water-fitness-long-beach). But underneath the rental-shop layer is a deeper, more competitive water culture that most visitors never see: masters swim teams that have been training the same lanes for decades, an outrigger canoe scene rooted in Hawaiian paddling tradition, and a stretch of water — **Marine Stadium** — good enough that it hosted rowing at the 1932 Olympics and will do it again for LA 2028. This is the guide to the part of Long Beach's water fitness world that isn't a rental kiosk.

## Masters Swim Clubs at Belmont Plaza (Belmont Shore)

**Belmont Plaza Olympic Pool** (4320 Olympic Plaza, Belmont Shore) is a $4-drop-in lap pool on its own, but it's also home to two of the most established masters swim programs in the region.

**Long Beach Grunions** run coached practices for every level from beginner to advanced: Monday and Wednesday 7–8 p.m., Friday 4–5 p.m., and Saturday 8–9:30 a.m., all at Belmont Pool. It's structured as much as a social club as a training team — a workout you show up to and stay a member of.

**SWIM Long Beach** trains adults from novice through competitive, including triathlon-specific work, with early sessions Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at 5:30 a.m. and 6:45 a.m. The club offers a first-three-sessions-free trial, which makes it a low-risk way to see whether coached masters swimming fits before committing to a season.

Both clubs share the pool's outdoor, sun-exposed 50-meter and 25-meter courses, and both are open to adults who just want structured coaching rather than solo lap swim. Drop-in lap swim remains available outside team hours for $4 if you'd rather swim on your own schedule.

## Outrigger Canoe Paddling at Mother's Beach (Naples Island)

If your image of a "paddle workout" stops at a solo SUP session, **Mother's Beach / Marine Park** (5865 E. Appian Way, Naples) runs a different program entirely: outrigger canoe.

**Kahakai Outrigger Canoe Club** and **Hui Lokahi O Ke Kai** both launch from Mother's Beach and welcome beginners regardless of age or paddling background — the one real requirement is the ability to swim. New paddlers typically try a session or two before deciding whether to join full-time. Kahakai also runs an offseason "Winter Lites" program, Saturday mornings from October through March, aimed at people who want to build a base before the competitive season or just stay active through the cooler months; non-members pay $100 for the full winter season.

Outrigger is a six-person team boat, which makes it one of the more social entries on this list — closer to joining a rec sports league than booking a rental. Kahakai also hosts an annual race at **Marine Stadium**, a short paddle from Mother's Beach.

## Scuba Diving: An Honest Update

Here's the closure we want to flag rather than paper over. **Deep Blue Scuba & Swim Center**, long the go-to dive shop at 11 39th Place near Belmont Pier, is marked closed as of its most recent update. The business's swim-lesson side relocated and now operates as **Deep Blue Swim School** in **Bixby Knolls** (3640 Atlantic Ave) — but that location teaches kids' and adult swim lessons, not scuba certification.

For scuba specifically, **Blue Holic Scuba** is the option currently operating out of Long Beach: a 5-star PADI center that departs from 320 Golden Shore via Catalina Express for real-ocean certification dives, with small-group instruction and extra support built in for nervous first-time divers. If you were counting on the old Belmont Shore shop, this is the honest redirect rather than a stale listing.

## Marine Stadium: Long Beach's Competitive Water Hub

**Marine Stadium** (5225 E Paoli Way) ties this whole guide together. Built for the 1932 Olympics as the first man-made rowing course in the U.S., it's been selected again as the official rowing venue for the **2028 LA Olympics** — which is a meaningful marker of how seriously this stretch of water is taken. Through the rest of 2026 it's also the site of the **MPSF Open Water Swimming Championships** (Saturday, October 17 — a 5K and 1K open water swim, open to spectators as well as competitors) and Kahakai's annual outrigger race. If you're building toward one of the masters swim programs above, watching the October championships at Marine Stadium is a free, low-pressure way to see what competitive open-water swimming actually looks like before signing up for one.

## The Corporate Wellness Angle

Team sports translate directly into workplace wellness, and water-based team activities have an advantage over gym-based ones: they're inherently low-impact, so they scale from the office triathlete to someone who hasn't done organized exercise in years. An outrigger canoe outing at Mother's Beach or a group masters swim session builds the kind of shared, low-injury-risk experience that a spin class or bootcamp can't always deliver for a mixed-fitness team.
