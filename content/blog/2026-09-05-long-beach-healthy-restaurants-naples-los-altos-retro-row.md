## Filling in the Rest of the Map

Our first healthy-restaurants guide back in June covered Belmont Shore, Belmont Heights, Downtown's East Village, Bixby Knolls, the Westside, and the PCH corridor near The Marketplace. Those spots — The Wild Chive, Poke Pola, Open Sesame, Higher Taste, Everytable, The Green Olive, PlanTita's Kitchen, Blue Bowl Superfoods, Veggie Grill — haven't changed. What's missing from that map is Naples Island, the Los Altos side of the Traffic Circle, the CSULB corridor, the Willmore District near Downtown, and Retro Row along 4th Street. This update fills in those five stretches with six verified spots.

## Naples Island: The Small Café

Tucked into the canal-front blocks at **5656 E 2nd St**, **The Small Café** has been a Naples fixture since 2000, now under longtime Long Beach resident ownership. It runs mornings only (7 a.m.–2 p.m.), which keeps it squarely a breakfast-and-brunch stop, but the menu carries real vegan and vegetarian options alongside the scrambles, and the small dog-friendly patio makes it an easy stop before or after a walk around the Naples canals. If you've already done our surf-and-paddle guide's Mother's Beach loop, this is the closest sit-down option to refuel.

## Los Altos: Simply Salad Near the Traffic Circle

Out past the 605/Traffic Circle interchange, **Simply Salad** (1775 Ximeno Ave, Ste B) runs a build-your-own model — pick a base, a protein, and toppings — open weekdays 10 a.m.–10 p.m. It's a chain concept, but it's one of the few build-your-own healthy counters on this side of the city, and it sits close enough to CSULB and the Los Altos residential grid to be a genuine weekday lunch option rather than a special trip.

## Near CSULB: Panini Kabob Grill on McGowen

A second Panini Kabob Grill location — distinct from the Downtown spot — sits at **4101 McGowen St, Ste 100**, in the shopping corridor east of the 405 near Cal State Long Beach. The chain's whole premise is "healthier Mediterranean fast-casual": grilled proteins, hummus, tabbouleh, and made-to-order paninis instead of fryer food, and this location keeps the same daily-fresh approach as the flagship. For anyone routing through the CSULB side of the city for a class, a run at the campus paths, or the Farmers Marketplace at Clark & Spring, it's the closest sit-down healthy option that isn't a chain smoothie counter.

## Downtown / Willmore District: moonbowls

At **1388 Daisy Ave**, on the Willmore District edge of Downtown, **moonbowls** builds Korean-inspired rice and grain bowls around bulgogi, tofu, and vegetable toppings — a different lane from the Broadway-corridor spots (Higher Taste, Everytable) covered in our original guide. It's open daily 11 a.m.–10 p.m., which makes it one of the later-closing healthy options in Downtown proper, useful if your schedule doesn't line up with an early dinner.

## Retro Row & Rose Park: The Grain Cafe and Rose Park Roasters

The 4th Street corridor east of Downtown — Retro Row into Rose Park — turns out to be one of the city's better-kept healthy-eating stretches. **The Grain Cafe** (4403 E 4th St) runs an all-day organic and vegan menu, open as early as 6 a.m. on weekdays through 10–11 p.m., built around grain bowls, smoothies, and plant-based versions of diner classics. A few blocks down at **3044 E 4th St**, **Rose Park Roasters** keeps things simpler — quality-sourced coffee and tea with a light, healthy-leaning food menu — a solid option for anyone doing the Retro Row antique-shop walk or ending a Rose Park loop with something other than a fast-food coffee.

## How This Fits Into a Healthy Long Beach Routine

The same pattern from our juice bar update holds here: the win isn't finding one "best" healthy restaurant, it's knowing which one sits on your actual route. Naples residents and anyone finishing a Mother's Beach morning have The Small Café. Los Altos and CSULB have Simply Salad and Panini Kabob Grill within a few minutes of campus. Downtown's Willmore edge has moonbowls as a later-hours option beyond the Broadway cluster. And the Retro Row/Rose Park stretch — already a farmers-market and antique-row destination — now has two sit-down healthy stops bookending the walk.

None of this requires a special trip. Pair The Small Café with a morning around the Naples canals. Pair Simply Salad or Panini Kabob Grill with a lap of the CSULB campus paths. Pair The Grain Cafe or Rose Park Roasters with the Thursday Fourth Street Farmers Market a few blocks away. The businesses in our original June guide are all still standing — this is genuinely additive, not a replacement.
