Long Beach has a certified farmers market almost every day of the week — we mapped all of them in our [day-by-day farmers market guide](/blog/2026-07-24-best-farmers-markets-long-beach). But markets run on their own schedule, and most of them are over by 2 p.m. If your week doesn't bend to a Tuesday afternoon in Alamitos Beach, the local-and-organic supply chain here runs a lot deeper than the market tents: working urban farms inside the city limits, subscription produce boxes, 400-plus rentable garden plots, and a set of natural grocers open from early morning to late night.

This is the second half of the local-food map — where to buy, grow, and pick up fresh food in Long Beach when the market isn't open. Fall is the right moment to sort it out: summer stone fruit is finished, and squash, apples, pomegranates, and persimmons are coming in.

## Urban Farms You Can Actually Buy From: Wrigley and North Long Beach

**Farm Lot 59** (2714 California Ave., on the **Wrigley** edge near Signal Hill) is the city's best-known urban farm — a nonprofit founded in 2010 by Sasha Kanno on a certified-organic plot that grows specialty cut flowers to subsidize fruit and vegetable crops donated to local food pantries. The farmstand sells bouquets, wreaths, and seeds, and the farm runs tours, gardening workshops, floral design classes, farm-to-table dinners, and standing volunteer days where you plant, harvest, wash, and pack alongside the crew. Farmstand hours have run Thursday through Sunday, 9 a.m.–1 p.m.; call (562) 542-8213 to confirm before you drive over, since a small farm's stand hours move with the harvest.

**The Growing Experience** (750 Via Carmelitos, **North Long Beach**) is the seven-acre farm tucked inside the Carmelitos public housing development — orchard, chickens, aquaponics, and over a hundred varieties of fruit, vegetables, and herbs grown without synthetic fertilizers or pesticides. Its published CSA runs $20 a box for non-residents and $10 for Carmelitos residents, with 8–10 seasonal items plus an herb, picked up Thursdays 3–6 p.m. or Fridays 2–5 p.m.; the public farm stand at the Carmelitos Senior Center (801 Via Carmelitos) has run Fridays 10 a.m.–noon, cash or check. One honest caveat: the farm's own produce page still carries a pandemic-era notice suspending the CSA and stand, and at least one major listing currently shows the site as closed. The operating history here is genuinely turbulent — LA County scaled it back in 2021 before the MAYE Center took over under a five-year land-use agreement in 2022. **Call (562) 984-2917 before you go.**

## Produce Boxes That Deliver to Long Beach

If nothing about a fixed pickup window works, a subscription box is the lowest-friction version of this habit. **Süprmarkt**, the Black-owned organic grocer out of South Central LA, delivers weekly boxes to parts of Long Beach along with the South Bay, Inglewood, Watts, and Compton; boxes have run about $30 weekly or $112 monthly, and the operation accepts EBT. For a same-city pickup instead, a **Weiser Family Farms** produce box can be pre-ordered and collected at **Alder & Sage** (366 Cherry Ave.) on Thursdays until 8 p.m. — the fallback for anyone who can't make the Fourth Street market's 8 a.m.–noon window on **Retro Row**.

## Grow It Yourself: Garden Plots in Zaferia, North Wrigley and East Long Beach

Long Beach has an unusually deep community-garden bench for a city this size, and a plot is cheaper than most produce habits.

**Long Beach Organic (LBO)** runs nine organic community gardens across the city and rents plots on six-month terms — March through August, and September through February, which means the fall cycle is opening right now. The largest is **Zaferia Junction Community Garden** (3709 E. 10th St., at 10th and Grand) with 90 plots, a large composting operation, a communal grape arbor, an orchard, and an earth oven. The newest is the **Captain Charles Moore Urban Community Garden** (3121 Long Beach Blvd., **North Wrigley**), with 30 plots plus four accessible raised beds, an orchard, a children's garden, a sensory garden, and a California native planting. Others sit at Orizaba Park, Grace Park, Crown Victory on Redondo Ave., Mary Molina, Seventh and Chestnut downtown, and Pacific and Sixth — the last of which reserves most plots for seniors at the neighboring Park Pacific Tower.

For something much bigger, the **Long Beach Community Garden Association** operates inside **El Dorado East Regional Park** in **East Long Beach** and bills itself as the largest community garden in Southern California: 303 plots at roughly 20′ × 30′ (600 sq ft) for **$160 per year**, with water access shared between every four plots, an on-site orchard whose fruit is shared among members, and monthly work parties the first Saturday of each month, 7:30–9:30 a.m. Year-round gardening is required — this is a commitment, not a novelty — but per square foot it's the cheapest organic produce in the city.

## Natural Grocers: Los Altos, Lakewood Village and Belmont Heights

For the nights when none of the above is open, Long Beach's organic grocery bench covers most of the map. **Lazy Acres Market** (2080 N. Bellflower Blvd., near **Los Altos** and CSULB) is open 6 a.m.–10 p.m. daily with organic produce, a scratch bakery and deli, and a juice bar inside. **Whole Foods Market** (3881 Lakewood Blvd., Ste. A, **Lakewood Village**) runs 7 a.m.–9 p.m. daily. **Sprouts Farmers Market** sits at 4600 E. Pacific Coast Hwy on the PCH corridor. And in **Belmont Heights**, **Olives Gourmet Grocer** (3510 E. Broadway) is the independent option — a neighborhood specialty grocer with a strong deli and prepared-food counter.
