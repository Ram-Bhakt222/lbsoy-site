There is a version of a Long Beach night out that doesn't cost you the next morning. It isn't staying home, and it isn't ordering a sad club soda with lime while everyone else gets a cocktail with three ingredients and a garnish. Over the last few years the city's bars have quietly built out real zero-proof programs — distilled non-alcoholic spirits, house syrups, the same care given to the drinks that come with alcohol — and the free summer programming on Pine Avenue and at Recreation Park does most of the work for you.

This is the honest map: where the mocktails are actually good in Long Beach, which venues make it easy to be the person not drinking, and what a genuinely fun alcohol-free night looks like here. One caveat up front — the area's only dedicated zero-proof bar, Shirley's Temple, closed permanently. So the answer in Long Beach isn't a single sober bar. It's knowing which regular bars take the non-drinker seriously.

## Downtown Long Beach: The Densest Cluster of Zero-Proof Menus

Pine Avenue is where to start, because you can walk between three of them.

**BO-beau kitchen + roof tap** (144 Pine Ave) has the most developed non-alcoholic list in the city. It leans on distilled zero-proof spirits rather than fruit juice, which is the whole difference — the Pink Madame builds Lyre's White Cane with lavender honey syrup and sparkling water, the Grapefruit & Berries Smash uses Ritual's whiskey alternative with citrus and house syrup, and the French Riviera Whip runs Lyre's Dry London through mango, pineapple, lime and coconut cream. The rooftop beer garden with the marquee lights is the draw, and nothing about ordering off the zero-proof side marks you as the odd one out. Open Wednesday–Thursday 5–9 p.m., Friday 5–10 p.m., Saturday 4–10 p.m., Sunday 4–9 p.m.; closed Monday and Tuesday.

**Anna's Joint** (443 Pine Ave, Ste A) is the low-key counterpart three blocks up — a neighborhood bar and deli that opens at 7 a.m. and runs to 10 or 11 p.m. Their non-alcoholic Negroni, built on zero-proof gin and vermouth, is the one to order; the bitter edge is what most mocktails miss.

**Olive & Rose** (255 Atlantic Ave) is the dressed-up option, a neo-bistro from the Heritage team inside a renovated motel on the edge of the East Village. The non-alcoholic drinks get the same culinary treatment as the food — the Herbal Remedy and the Avo Verdita are savory rather than sweet, which matters if you're three hours into an evening. Tuesday–Thursday 5–9 p.m., Friday–Saturday 5–10 p.m.; closed Sunday and Monday.

Down on the water, **Parkers' Lighthouse** (435 Shoreline Village Dr) runs the most-mentioned mocktail program in Long Beach, built on Ritual Zero Proof: the Spicy Señorita with jalapeño heat, grapefruit and a Tajín rim, the Lavender Paradise with a gin-free spirit, elderflower tonic and honeyed lavender, plus the Blood Orange Bomb and Cucumber Crush. The harbor view from the lighthouse is worth the walk from the Shoreline path. Open daily from 11:30 a.m., to 9 p.m. Sunday–Thursday and 10 p.m. Friday–Saturday.

## Retro Row and East Anaheim: The Neighborhood Options

**Baby Gee** (1227 E 4th St) sits in the 4th Street Corridor and is the cozy-speakeasy end of the spectrum. The zero-proof list — White Lightning, Suffusion of Yellow — is short but deliberate, and the room is small enough that a good non-alcoholic drink actually reads as a choice. Open every day, from 5 p.m. Monday–Friday and 2 p.m. on weekends, late Thursday through Saturday.

**The Bamboo Club** (3522 E Anaheim St) is the sleeper pick. Tiki bars are usually the hardest place to not drink — the drinks *are* the point — but Bamboo makes alcohol-free versions of its signature tiki cocktails, which means you get the whole ritual: the mug, the garnish, the fruit and spice layering. Open Monday–Tuesday 3–10 p.m., Wednesday–Thursday to midnight, Friday–Saturday 11 a.m.–2 a.m., Sunday 11 a.m.–10 p.m.

Out toward **Belmont Shore**, **The Bungalow** at 2ND & PCH publishes a standing Zero Proof menu covering both non-alcoholic spirits and beers — useful to know before you walk into an indoor-outdoor bar that size.

## Healthy Nightlife in Long Beach That Isn't in a Bar At All

The other half of the sober-curious question is what to *do* after dark, and Long Beach's best evening programming happens to be free and alcohol-optional by design.

**Live at the Shell** runs Tuesdays 6–8 p.m. at the historic 1929 band shell in Recreation Park through August 25 — free, all ages, bring a blanket and a picnic. **Moonlight Movies on the Beach** finishes its season at Granada Beach with a *Grease* sing-a-long, and it's explicitly a no-alcohol event, which quietly makes it one of the easiest nights out in the city. **Taste of Downtown with KCRW Summer Nights** takes over Pine Avenue on Saturday, August 22 from 2 to 10 p.m. — free entry, KCRW DJs, and a dance floor that keeps going after sundown. And **Stroll & Savor** turns 2nd Street in Belmont Shore into a walking food festival on August 19–20; ticket sheets run $15 for 12 or $20 for 18, and the whole thing is a two-mile walk you barely notice taking.

For a standing option: **Yoga on the Bluff** above Alamitos Beach adds a 6 p.m. weekday session through October. Free, outdoors, and a much better hour to spend than the one you'd spend at happy hour.

## The Practical Version

If you're doing a dry month, or just want to drive home clear-headed, the move in Long Beach is to pick the venue first and the drink second. Call ahead if the zero-proof menu matters — programs change seasonally, and a bar with three distilled non-alcoholic spirits behind the counter will treat you completely differently than one that reaches for cranberry juice.

Worth watching in January: the Downtown Long Beach Alliance runs **The Great Mocktail Mixer**, a Dry January mocktail crawl through DTLB venues. It's the single best night to survey the whole scene at once.

None of this is medical advice, and it isn't a lecture about drinking. It's just that a night that ends with a clear head tends to make the next morning's walk on the bluff considerably more likely — and most of the good habits in this city compound that way.
