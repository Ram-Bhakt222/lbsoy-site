Few cities pack as much yoga into one zip-code cluster as Long Beach. You can practice on the grass above the Pacific for free at 11 a.m., sweat through a Bikram class on Pacific Coast Highway in the afternoon, and finish with a donation-based sound bath downtown that same night. Whether you're brand new to a mat or you've been practicing for years, this guide covers the best yoga and movement studios in Long Beach for 2026 — organized by neighborhood, with what makes each one worth the drive.

A quick note on language before we start: most of what's below is community yoga — group yoga classes led by an experienced yoga teacher, open to every level. That's different from clinical **yoga therapy**, a one-on-one, evidence-based practice for specific health conditions, which we cover separately on our [yoga therapy](/yoga-therapy) page. Both belong in a healthy Long Beach life; they just serve different needs.

## Free & Donation-Based Yoga: The Bluff and Downtown

The single best-known yoga experience in Long Beach costs nothing. **Yoga on the Bluff**, run by **Yogalution Movement & Wellness**, meets every day at 11 a.m. on the grassy bluff at Ocean Boulevard and Junipero Avenue in **Alamitos Beach**, with a second sunset session on weekdays at 6 p.m. through October. Classes are all-levels, all-ages, led by rotating teachers, and free — donations welcome but never required, and the only thing that cancels a session is rain or wet ground. It's the easiest way to start a practice in this city, and locals treat it as a daily ritual.

Yogalution's brick-and-mortar home base sits nearby at 244 Redondo Avenue and is Long Beach's only fully donation-based studio, so the pay-what-you-can ethos carries indoors too. Downtown, **Yoga108** at 242 East 3rd Street in the **East Village Arts District** runs low-cost community classes alongside its regular schedule, plus periodic sound baths and kirtans. Between the two, you can build a consistent practice in Long Beach for close to nothing — a rarity in Southern California.

## Downtown & East Village: Yoga108

**Yoga108** is the anchor studio for anyone living or working in **Downtown Long Beach**. Located a half-block from Harvey Milk Promenade Park (with the first two hours of parking free), it runs daily vinyasa classes for beginners through advanced practitioners, plus HIIT and circuit-style movement classes for people who want a stronger sweat, slower meditative sessions with sound bath, and a full teacher-training track. The range means a downtown desk worker can drop in for a lunchtime flow, then bring a friend to a restorative evening class the same week. For the dense office core of Downtown and the East Village, it's the most convenient serious studio in the city.

## Belmont Shore & the Marina: Ra Yoga, West Coast Sweat, Purple Yoga

The Pacific Coast Highway corridor toward **Belmont Shore** and Marina Pacifica is Long Beach's densest cluster of full-service studios.

**Ra Yoga** (3860 Worsham Avenue) is the polished, full-amenity option: a 3,400-square-foot space with two large practice rooms, a dedicated meditation room, full locker rooms, and eco-friendly radiant heat rather than forced-air heaters. The schedule spans vinyasa, hot yoga, yin and restorative, prenatal, strength, and sound-bath meditation — and, notably, the studio offers childcare during most morning classes, which makes it one of the few genuinely parent-friendly options in the area.

**West Coast Sweat** (6324 East PCH, in Marina Pacifica) is the hot-yoga institution. Open since 2002 as the original hot-yoga studio in the area, it runs roughly 30 classes a week — authentic Bikram, Hot Yoga Fusion, and Inferno Hot Pilates, a HIIT-style class built on Pilates principles — for people who want heat and intensity. If your idea of a good class ends with your mat soaked, this is your studio.

**Purple Yoga** (6535 East PCH) rounds out the corridor with a community-first, longevity-minded approach and a welcoming feel for beginners. It's a good landing spot if the hot-yoga studios feel intimidating and you'd rather ease in.

## North Long Beach & Bixby Knolls: FreedOhm Wellness

Up the **Long Beach Boulevard** corridor toward **Bixby Knolls**, **FreedOhm Wellness** (4647 Long Beach Blvd) takes a broader wellness-center approach, blending yoga and meditation classes with Reiki and empowerment coaching. It's a smaller, more intimate room than the PCH studios — a fit for people on the north side of the city who want a calm, personal space rather than a high-volume schedule.

## How to Choose a Studio in Long Beach

If you're brand new, start free: a few weeks of Yoga on the Bluff or a low-cost community class at Yoga108 will tell you whether you like flow, heat, or something slower before you commit to a membership. If you want heat and a hard workout, West Coast Sweat or Ra Yoga's hot classes are the picks. If budget is the constraint, Yogalution's donation model is unmatched. And if you're a parent, Ra Yoga's morning childcare quietly solves the biggest barrier to a regular practice. Most studios offer a discounted intro week, so it's cheap to sample two or three before deciding.
