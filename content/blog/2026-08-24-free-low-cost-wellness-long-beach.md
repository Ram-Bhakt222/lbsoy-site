The most common reason people give for not taking care of themselves is money. In Long Beach, that reason holds up worse than almost anywhere else in Southern California. Between a city parks department that runs free outdoor gyms, a donation-based studio that has held a free daily session on the bluff for years, a nonprofit clinic with sliding-scale pricing, free museum days, and a library card that gets you into state parks, it is genuinely possible to build a serious weekly wellness routine here for close to nothing.

Here is the verified version of that — real places, real prices, checked as of August 2026.

## Free Outdoor Gyms: The City's Fitness Zones

Long Beach Parks, Recreation and Marine operates a network of **Fitness Zones** — free, open-air exercise equipment installed in public parks, paired with walking paths and posted instruction stations. There is no membership, no sign-up, and no hours beyond the park's own.

The largest is the **Downtown Fitness Loop**, roughly 4.8 miles of path with pull-up bars, dip stations, and body-weight equipment spaced along it. The **Bixby Fitness Loop** at Junipero and Ocean gives you six stations with an ocean view. **Houghton Park** in North Long Beach has a half-mile loop with bilingual signage and QR codes that pull up demonstration videos, which makes it the best option if you have never used this kind of equipment before. ADA-accessible sets are installed at **Lincoln Park**, **14th Street Park**, **Orizaba Park**, and **Cesar E. Chavez Park**.

If your budget for fitness is zero, this is the floor — and it is a surprisingly good floor.

## Free Daily Yoga on the Bluff — Alamitos Beach

**Yogalution Movement & Wellness** (244 Redondo Ave) runs a free outdoor session at **11 a.m., seven days a week, year-round**, on the grass overlooking Junipero Beach at Ocean Boulevard and Junipero Avenue. Donations are accepted but never required. From April through October, they add sunset sessions at 6 p.m. on weekdays — which means the evening option is running right now and will be through the fall.

All ages and experience levels are welcome, including beginners, kids, and leashed dogs. The studio itself is donation-based, which makes it one of the few indoor movement spaces in the city with no fixed price of entry.

## Sliding-Scale Care: Urban Salt in the Zaferia District

**Urban Salt Community Acupuncture Clinic** (1703 Termino Ave) is Long Beach's only 501(c)(3) community acupuncture clinic. Treatments run **$45**, with an optional **$55** tier where the extra money funds free clinic hours for people who cannot pay. Hours are Monday through Friday 9:30 a.m.–8 p.m. and Saturday–Sunday 9 a.m.–1 p.m. They also run sliding-scale community health education classes.

Worth knowing: the previous tenant at that address, Long Beach Community Acupuncture, has permanently closed — if you find that listing online, it is stale.

## Free Museum Days: MOLAA in the East Village

The **Museum of Latin American Art** (628 Alamitos Ave) is **free every Sunday**, all day, and free on the **fourth Friday of every month from 5 to 9 p.m.** Pre-booking a ticket online is encouraged, but walk-ins are welcome. It is a real, unhurried indoor walk with a sculpture garden attached — a good rainy-day or heat-wave substitute for the beach path.

## Your Library Card Is a Wellness Card

Two underused programs at the **Long Beach Public Library**:

- **California State Parks Pass** — check out a free vehicle day-use pass with your library card, good at participating state parks. That turns a tank of gas into a full hiking day.
- **Discover & Go** — reserve free or discounted admission to museums, gardens, and cultural sites through the library's website.

Both cost nothing beyond having a card, which is also free.

## Free & Low-Cost City Classes

The Parks, Recreation and Marine department is offering **more than 700 classes, activities, sports and leagues** this fall, running September through November, for ages preschool through 50-and-over. Registration opened August 3 and popular sections fill fast. Beyond the fee-based classes, the **Recreation Connection** catalog lists dozens of genuinely free activities — youth sports leagues, teen centers, and senior centers among them. The catalog is online at LBParks.org and in print at park community centers and library branches; the department line is 562-570-3111.

## Eat Better for Less: Market Match

Long Beach has a certified farmers market almost every day of the week, and at participating markets the **Market Match** program doubles CalFresh/EBT spending — for every dollar you spend with an EBT card, you get up to **$15 in additional free scrip** for fruits and vegetables per visit. Ask at the market information booth. WIC benefits are matched at many sites as well.

## Membership Help: YMCA of Greater Long Beach

If you want an indoor facility and the price is the obstacle, the **YMCA of Greater Long Beach** runs an income-based financial assistance program covering both memberships and programs. You download and complete the forms, submit through your local branch, and allow up to 15 business days for processing. Assistance is reassessed annually.

## The Free Infrastructure You Already Own

Beyond any program: the **Shoreline Pedestrian Bikepath** gives you about 4.5 flat, car-free miles from Downtown to **Belmont Shore**. The **Bluff Park** loop above Alamitos Beach is a mile-plus of ocean-side walking. The **El Dorado Nature Center** in **East Long Beach** is free to enter on foot or by bike — only cars pay ($6–9, cashless) — for 2-plus miles of dirt and paved trail. **Hilltop Park** in **Signal Hill** is a short, steep 206-foot climb to a harbor-and-Catalina view. None of these charge a person to walk in.

And free at any hour: the **988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline** (call or text 988), the **Crisis Text Line** (text "LA" to 741741), and the **NAMI HelpLine** (1-800-950-6264). This is general information rather than a treatment recommendation — for an ongoing condition, a licensed provider is the right next step.

## A Note on What Low-Cost Can and Cannot Do

Free and low-cost options are excellent at building the habit — showing up, moving, eating better, being outside with other people. They are not a substitute for individualized care when there is a real health condition in the picture. If you are working around chronic pain, a recent surgery, a cardiac or respiratory diagnosis, or a pregnancy, the useful step is an assessment with a credentialed yoga therapist or another qualified clinician before you build a routine around general-population programming.
